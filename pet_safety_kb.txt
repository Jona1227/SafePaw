PET SAFETY KNOWLEDGE BASE
Each entry describes one food, plant, or household item and how it affects common pets.
If a pet has been exposed and is showing symptoms, contact a veterinarian or the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center (888-426-4435) right away.

==========

Item: Chocolate
Category: Food
Pets affected: Dogs (high), cats (high)
Toxicity: High
Toxic component: Theobromine and caffeine; dark and baking chocolate are the most dangerous
Symptoms: Vomiting, diarrhea, restlessness, rapid heartbeat, tremors, seizures
What to do: Contact a vet or animal poison control right away; note the type and amount eaten

==========

Item: Grapes and Raisins
Category: Food
Pets affected: Dogs (high), cats (possible)
Toxicity: High
Toxic component: Tartaric acid is the suspected cause; even small amounts can be dangerous
Symptoms: Vomiting, lethargy, loss of appetite, decreased urination, kidney failure
What to do: Contact a vet right away, even if no symptoms are showing yet

==========

Item: Xylitol (sugar-free gum, candy, some peanut butters)
Category: Food
Pets affected: Dogs (high)
Toxicity: High
Toxic component: Xylitol causes a rapid insulin release in dogs
Symptoms: Vomiting, weakness, staggering, seizures, low blood sugar, liver failure
What to do: Contact a vet immediately; check product labels for xylitol or "birch sugar"

==========

Item: Onions and Garlic
Category: Food
Pets affected: Cats (high), dogs (moderate to high)
Toxicity: High
Toxic component: Compounds that damage red blood cells; raw, cooked, and powdered forms are all toxic
Symptoms: Weakness, pale gums, vomiting, rapid breathing, anemia (may appear days later)
What to do: Contact a vet; keep onion- and garlic-seasoned foods away from pets

==========

Item: Macadamia Nuts
Category: Food
Pets affected: Dogs (moderate)
Toxicity: Moderate
Toxic component: Unknown
Symptoms: Weakness (especially back legs), vomiting, tremors, fever
What to do: Contact a vet; symptoms usually appear within 12 hours

==========

Item: Alcohol
Category: Food
Pets affected: Dogs (high), cats (high)
Toxicity: High
Toxic component: Ethanol (also found in raw bread dough)
Symptoms: Vomiting, disorientation, difficulty breathing, low body temperature, coma
What to do: Contact a vet or animal poison control right away

==========

Item: Caffeine (coffee, tea, energy drinks)
Category: Food
Pets affected: Dogs (high), cats (high)
Toxicity: High
Toxic component: Methylxanthines

==========

What to do: For cats, contact a vet immediately, even after small exposures

Item: Ibuprofen and Naproxen (NSAIDs)
Category: Household (medication)
Pets affected: Dogs (high), cats (high)
Toxicity: High
Toxic component: Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs
Symptoms: Vomiting, bloody stool, stomach ulcers, kidney failure
What to do: Contact a vet immediately; never give human pain medication to pets

==========

Item: Acetaminophen (Tylenol)
Category: Household (medication)
Pets affected: Cats (severe), dogs (high)
Toxicity: High
Toxic component: Acetaminophen; cats cannot break it down safely
Symptoms: Brown or gray gums, difficulty breathing, facial swelling, liver damage
What to do: Contact a vet immediately; a single tablet can be fatal to a cat

==========

Item: Antifreeze (ethylene glycol)
Category: Household (chemical)
Pets affected: Dogs (high), cats (high)
Toxicity: High
Toxic component: Ethylene glycol; it tastes sweet, which attracts pets
Symptoms: Staggering, vomiting, excessive thirst, seizures, kidney failure
What to do: Contact a vet immediately; treatment works best within hours

==========

Item: Rodent Poison (rodenticides)
Category: Household (chemical)
Pets affected: Dogs (high), cats (high)
Toxicity: High
Toxic component: Varies by product (anticoagulants, bromethalin, cholecalciferol)
Symptoms: Bleeding, bruising, weakness, tremors, seizures (depends on the type)
What to do: Contact a vet immediately and bring the product packaging

==========

Item: Dog Flea and Tick Products (permethrin) on Cats
Category: Household (medication)
Pets affected: Cats (severe)
Toxicity: High
Toxic component: Permethrin, found in many dog-only spot-on treatments
Symptoms: Tremors, twitching, seizures, drooling
What to do: Contact a vet immediately; never use dog flea products on cats

==========

Item: Vitamin D (supplements, some psoriasis creams)
Category: Household (medication)
Pets affected: Dogs (high), cats (high)
Toxicity: High
Toxic component: Vitamin D (cholecalciferol) and related drugs like calcipotriene, which raise blood calcium
Symptoms: Vomiting, loss of appetite, excessive thirst and urination, weakness, kidney failure
What to do: Contact a vet immediately; signs can take a day or more to appear

==========

Item: Iron Supplements (including prenatal vitamins)
Category: Household (medication)
Pets affected: Dogs (moderate to high), cats (moderate to high)
Toxicity: Moderate to High
Toxic component: Elemental iron
Symptoms: Vomiting (sometimes with blood), diarrhea, lethargy, abdominal pain, liver damage
What to do: Contact a vet right away and bring the bottle so the iron amount can be checked

==========

Item: ADHD Medications (amphetamines, methylphenidate)
Category: Household (medication)
Pets affected: Dogs (high), cats (high)
Toxicity: High
Toxic component: Stimulants
Symptoms: Hyperactivity, rapid heartbeat, panting, tremors, high body temperature, seizures
What to do: Contact a vet immediately

==========

Item: Antidepressants (SSRIs, SNRIs)
Category: Household (medication)
Pets affected: Dogs (moderate to high), cats (moderate to high)
Toxicity: Moderate to High
Toxic component: Antidepressant drugs; some cats are attracted to venlafaxine capsules
Symptoms: Sleepiness or agitation, vomiting, wobbliness, tremors, rapid heartbeat, seizures
What to do: Contact a vet right away

==========

Item: Sleep Aids (zolpidem, benzodiazepines)
Category: Household (medication)
Pets affected: Dogs (moderate), cats (moderate)
Toxicity: Moderate
Toxic component: Sedative drugs
Symptoms: Heavy sleepiness, wobbliness, or the opposite effect of agitation and hyperactivity
What to do: Contact a vet

==========

Item: Cold and Allergy Decongestants (pseudoephedrine, phenylephrine)
Category: Household (medication)
Pets affected: Dogs (high), cats (high)
Toxicity: High
Toxic component: Decongestants; often found in "D" versions of allergy medications
Symptoms: Agitation, rapid heartbeat, high blood pressure, high body temperature, tremors, seizures
What to do: Contact a vet immediately; check the label for a decongestant ingredient

==========

Item: Antihistamines (diphenhydramine, loratadine, cetirizine)
Category: Household (medication)
Pets affected: Dogs (low to moderate), cats (low to moderate)
Toxicity: Low to Moderate
Toxic component: Antihistamines; risk rises with large amounts or combination products that include a decongestant
Symptoms: Sleepiness or hyperactivity, rapid heartbeat, dry mouth, vomiting
What to do: Contact a vet, especially if a large amount or a combination product was eaten

==========

Item: Flavored Pet Medications (chewable tablets)
Category: Household (medication)
Pets affected: Dogs (varies), cats (varies)
Toxicity: Varies
Toxic component: Overdose of the pet's own medication; flavored chews may lead pets to eat a whole bottle
Symptoms: Depends on the drug; can include vomiting, diarrhea, stomach ulcers, or kidney or liver damage
What to do: Contact a vet and bring the medication bottle; store pet meds out of reach

==========

Item: Prescription Skin Creams (fluorouracil, calcipotriene)
Category: Household (medication)
Pets affected: Dogs (severe), cats (severe)
Toxicity: High
Toxic component: Fluorouracil (5-FU) and calcipotriene; even a small amount licked from a tube can be serious
Symptoms: Vomiting, tremors, seizures (fluorouracil); high calcium and kidney failure (calcipotriene)
What to do: Contact a vet immediately

==========

Item: Kratom
Category: Household (recreational drug)
Pets affected: Dogs (moderate), cats (moderate)
Toxicity: Moderate
Toxic component: Mitragynine and related compounds
Symptoms: Vomiting, sleepiness or agitation, wobbliness, tremors
What to do: Contact a vet

==========

Item: Marijuana and THC Edibles
Category: Household (recreational drug)
Pets affected: Dogs (moderate), cats (moderate)
Toxicity: Moderate
Toxic component: THC; edibles may also contain chocolate or xylitol
Symptoms: Wobbliness, dribbling urine, lethargy, sensitivity to sound and touch, slow heartbeat
What to do: Contact a vet; tell them honestly what was eaten so they can treat it correctly

==========

Item: Wild Mushrooms
Category: Plant
Pets affected: Dogs (high), cats (high)
Toxicity: High (varies by species)
Toxic component: Varies; some species, such as Amanita, contain toxins that destroy the liver
Symptoms: Vomiting, diarrhea, drooling, wobbliness, tremors, seizures, liver failure (may be delayed)
What to do: Contact a vet immediately; bring a photo or sample of the mushroom if possible

==========

Category: Household (chemical)
Pets affected: Dogs (high), cats (high)
Toxicity: High
Toxic component: Nicotine; some nicotine gum also contains xylitol
Symptoms: Vomiting, drooling, agitation, rapid heartbeat, tremors, seizures

==========

Item: Avocado
Category: Food
Pets affected: Birds, rabbits, horses, goats and sheep (high); dogs and cats (mild)
Toxicity: Low for dogs and cats; High for birds, rabbits and livestock
Toxic component: Persin; the pit is also a choking and blockage risk
Symptoms: Dogs and cats: mild vomiting or diarrhea. Birds and rabbits: breathing problems, heart damage
What to do: For dogs and cats, monitor and contact a vet if symptoms appear or the pit was swallowed; for birds and rabbits, contact a vet immediately

Toxic component: Sodium; large amounts cause sodium ion poisoning

Item: Raw Yeast Dough
Category: Food
Pets affected: Dogs (high), cats (high)
Toxicity: High
Toxic component: Yeast, which expands in the stomach and produces alcohol

Item: Citrus (stems, leaves, peels, seeds)
Category: Food
Pets affected: Dogs (low to moderate), cats (low to moderate)
Toxicity: Low to Moderate
Toxic component: Citric acid and essential oils, mostly in the plant parts and peel rather than the fruit
Symptoms: Vomiting, diarrhea, skin irritation; large amounts can cause depression of the nervous system
What to do: Contact a vet if a large amount was eaten or symptoms appear

==========

Item: Cooked Bones
Category: Food
Pets affected: Dogs (moderate to high), cats (moderate to high)
Toxicity: Not toxic, but a physical hazard
Toxic component: None; cooked bones can splinter
Symptoms: Choking, gagging, vomiting, constipation, bloody stool, signs of a blocked or punctured gut
What to do: Contact a vet if your pet shows any of these signs after eating a bone
